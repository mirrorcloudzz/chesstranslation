5 Focal-points  
<!-- AA-p080-l002 -->
The series of examples examined in the chapter on mating patterns has given us a  
<!-- AA-p080-l003 -->
good idea of the difficulties a defender encounters when his forces (especially  
<!-- AA-p080-l004 -->
the pawns in front of his castled king) are poorly placed and when the attacker's  
<!-- AA-p080-l005 -->
one aim is to fill the various 'holes and cracks' in the weakened enemy defences  
<!-- AA-p080-l006 -->
with his own pieces and thus obtain an even tighter grip. It follows from this that  
<!-- AA-p080-l007 -->
the squares in the immediate vicinity of the king, where the drama of the mating  
<!-- AA-p080-l008 -->
attack usually reaches its climax, play a special role. If the attacker threatens  
<!-- AA-p080-l009 -->
mate or actually mates on these squares, they are mating focal-points, but if he  
<!-- AA-p080-l010 -->
only harries the king from them or uses them as points from which to break into  
<!-- AA-p080-l011 -->
the castled position, they are called strategic or auxiliary focal-points. It may be  
<!-- AA-p080-l012 -->
that there is more than one mating focal-point, and in that case we speak of com-  
<!-- AA-p080-l013 -->
pound focal-points. If there are many focal-points (both mating and strategic) on  
<!-- AA-p080-l014 -->
squares of the same colour, we speak of a network of weak squares.  
<!-- AA-p080-l015 -->
Generally speaking, every focal-point is a weak square in the defender's terri-  
<!-- AA-p080-l016 -->
tory and a potentially strong one for the attacker. In addition to the focal-points  
<!-- AA-p080-l017 -->
there are also other squares in the castling area which have a specific function,  
<!-- AA-p080-l018 -->
e.g. blocked squares, particularly those occupied by the defender's pawns, and  
<!-- AA-p080-l019 -->
also any weak squares on which the attacker can safely post his pieces.  
<!-- AA-p080-l020 -->
