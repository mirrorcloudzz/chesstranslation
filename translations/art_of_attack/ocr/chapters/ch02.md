2 The attack on the king that has  
<!-- AA-p028-l002 -->
lost the right to castle  
<!-- AA-p028-l003 -->
This situation arises when the king is either no longer able to castle or has been  
<!-- AA-p028-l004 -->
driven by the opponent away from the castling area. In spite of these possible dif-  
<!-- AA-p028-l005 -->
ferences of origin, the general picture and the problem involved are identical and  
<!-- AA-p028-l006 -->
ought to be dealt with in one and the same chapter.  
<!-- AA-p028-l007 -->
First of all, it must be emphasized that the actual fact that a king has lost the  
<!-- AA-p028-l008 -->
right to castle does not necessarily always justify undertaking an attack aimed at  
<!-- AA-p028-l009 -->
mate (as will be shown by the examples of the voluntary loss of castling rights in  
<!-- AA-p028-l010 -->
chapter three). For an attack of this kind to be feasible, the loss of castling rights  
<!-- AA-p028-l011 -->
must at the same time involve the exposure of the king and an increased vulnera--  
<!-- AA-p028-l012 -->
bility to attack. In the majority of cases the attack is in fact in order, and we shall  
<!-- AA-p028-l013 -->
only consider such positive cases and not the rest. It only remains to be added that  
<!-- AA-p028-l014 -->
the loss of the right to castle can also entail a further weakness besides the danger  
<!-- AA-p028-l015 -->
which faces the king; that is, communication between the rooks is made more  
<!-- AA-p028-l016 -->
difficult. In fact, an attack may logically direct itself against this lack of co-  
<!-- AA-p028-l017 -->
operation between the rooks, e.g. in a struggle for control of an open file.  
<!-- AA-p028-l018 -->
We shall first examine a few typical examples portraying the drama of the king  
<!-- AA-p028-l019 -->
which has lost the right to castle. Such a drama can be divided up into the follow-  
<!-- AA-p028-l020 -->
ing episodes:  
<!-- AA-p028-l021 -->
1) spoiling the king's castling chances, or drawing it away from the castling  
<!-- AA-p028-l022 -->
position;  
<!-- AA-p028-l023 -->
2) the pursuit of the king across the board by checking; and  
<!-- AA-p028-l024 -->
3) the final mating attack in the middle of the board or on the edge.  
<!-- AA-p028-l025 -->

<!-- AA-p033-l012 -->
