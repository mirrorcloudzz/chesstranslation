The attack on the fianchettoed king position  
<!-- AA-p231-l010 -->

<!-- AA-p231-l011 -->
being developed on the flank (on g2, b2, g7 or b7), and if a player castles behind  
<!-- AA-p231-l012 -->

<!-- AA-p231-l013 -->

<!-- AA-p231-l014 -->

<!-- AA-p231-l015 -->
which occurs in the Dragon Variation of the Sicilian, and the King's Indian and  
<!-- AA-p231-l016 -->
Grünfeld defences.  
<!-- AA-p231-l017 -->

<!-- AA-p231-l018 -->

<!-- AA-p231-l019 -->

<!-- AA-p231-l020 -->

<!-- AA-p231-l021 -->

<!-- AA-p231-l022 -->

<!-- AA-p231-l028 -->

The attack on the queenside castled position  
<!-- AA-p236-l019 -->
Before discussing the attack on the queenside castled position, one should first of  
<!-- AA-p236-l020 -->
all examine the differences between this type of castling and kingside castling.  
<!-- AA-p236-l021 -->

<!-- AA-p236-l022 -->

<!-- AA-p236-l023 -->

<!-- AA-p236-l024 -->
castled position only in this respect, that the positions of the king and rook are  
<!-- AA-p236-l025 -->
both a square nearer the centre of the board. This can be useful for the rook, but it  
<!-- AA-p236-l026 -->
usually presents difficulties to the king, which is safer on gl than cl. Reckoning
