4 Mating patterns  
<!-- AA-p066-l002 -->
Spatial relationships in a mating attack can be observed by examining mating  
<!-- AA-p066-l003 -->
patterns, focal-points, and the roles played by the ranks, files, and diagonals. In  
<!-- AA-p066-l004 -->
this chapter we shall be concerned with mating patterns. This choice has a practi-  
<!-- AA-p066-l005 -->
cal justification in that mating patterns can be more easily remembered than the  
<!-- AA-p066-l006 -->
ways leading up to mate. For this reason mating patterns, even though they occur  
<!-- AA-p066-l007 -->
only at the very end of a mating attack, are fundamental for practical purposes  
<!-- AA-p066-l008 -->
because of the excellent mnemonic assistance which they provide to the player.  
<!-- AA-p066-l009 -->
Our first concern is for the most common mating patterns; these hardly ever  
<!-- AA-p066-l010 -->
occur in the middle of the board, but they are very frequent in the castling area,  
<!-- AA-p066-l011 -->
i.e. near the king's position on the edge of the board.  
<!-- AA-p066-l012 -->
Typical mates without enemy pieces  
<!-- AA-p066-l013 -->
There are a very large number of typical mates of this kind and most of them are  
<!-- AA-p066-l014 -->
elementary, so we shall consider only a few examples. (For the sake of greater  
<!-- AA-p066-l015 -->
clarity the diagrams are shown without the white king.)  
<!-- AA-p066-l016 -->

<!-- AA-p067-l017 -->

Damiano'sMate  
<!-- AA-p068-l005 -->
This is a position from the book (1512) by Damiano, a Portuguese apothecary,  
<!-- AA-p068-l006 -->
one of the first authors in the literature of European chess. White mates in five  
<!-- AA-p068-l007 -->
moves.  
<!-- AA-p068-l008 -->
1 h8+ xh8 2 h1+ g8 3 h8+ xh8 4 Wh1+ g8 5 Wh7#.  
<!-- AA-p068-l009 -->
The rook sacrifices were naturally introduced to make the essential point  
<!-- AA-p068-l010 -->
clear: the queen must reach the h-file with gain of tempo.  
<!-- AA-p068-l011 -->
The pattern of Damiano's mate can be seen more clearly in the following ex-  
<!-- AA-p068-l012 -->
amples.

<!-- AA-p073-l008 -->

<!-- AA-p073-l009 -->

<!-- AA-p073-l010 -->
